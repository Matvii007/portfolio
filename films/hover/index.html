01 The idea

The 3 p.m. slump, played as a meeting.

HOVER is an invented sparkling cold brew, and the brief I gave myself was the afternoon slump everyone has sat through.

A boardroom where everyone has melted into their chairs and the steam pours down from the mugs instead of up. One person is still awake. She doesn’t argue — she slides a can across the table. One sip, and the slump turns into a rush you can see: the light, the tie, the papers, the work.

03 Previs in Blender

Every shot is blocked before it’s generated.

Each scene starts as a grey Blender blockout: the long boardroom table and who sits where, real lenses from 28 to 70 mm, every camera move, and the moments that matter timed to the frame — the sigh, the slide, the rush, the lob.

The blockout is the motion reference for the video model. Even the tired people move in it — rubbing eyes, nodding off, yawning — because a still blockout gives you statues.

04 Cast, can and world

One can, drawn the same in every shot.

Maya, Leo, Tom, Nora, the boardroom and the office are designed once, as reference sheets. The can is one simple design — a big vertical wordmark and a coffee bean, nothing else printed — and that single image goes into every frame that shows it, so the label never drifts.

05 Keyframes

23 keyframes, approved before anything moved.

A frame for every camera change and every key state, made with GPT Image 2. Each frame is built from the previous one in the same room, so the furniture, the mugs and who sits where never jump between cuts. Cold overcast light until the sip, real warm sun after it. Click any frame to open it.

06 Directing the model

Prompts written like a shot list.

One generation per scene, several shots cut inside a single take, each with its camera move and an end state. The storyboard frames and the can go in as references — no character portraits, which the model likes to paste in as a still. Physics and pacing are written out: real-time speed, one can, the hand travels with the can.

Keyframe B6 · GPT Image 2 · 16:9 
Same low front-left angle, waist-up, a moment later: Leo (man, 32, short tousled brown hair, light stubble, white shirt with rolled sleeves and a slim navy tie) has lowered the can and holds the HOVER can at his chest; his eyes are wide open, eyebrows up, a stunned, euphoric smile spreading, as if a rush of happiness just hit his head; his hair and tie are lifted back by a soft wave of air and a few papers float up from the table. He stays seated. Warm real sunlight floods the boardroom. 28 mm. Same realistic cinematic photography as the cold scenes: ARRI Alexa, anamorphic lens, natural skin texture, fine film grain. Only the light has changed: warm late-afternoon sunlight now streams through the windows as a golden backlight, warmer white balance, richer natural colours in the room, crisp contrast. Realistic, not stylised: no fantasy sky, no glow or light effects, no oversaturation.
  • Chain — built from the previous frame of the same take, so the room is identical.
  • Performance — the body’s reaction, not a pose: no arms-up celebration.
  • Product — the can stays in his hand, label to camera.
  • Light — the same photography as the cold scenes; only the sun changes.

Realistic warm light after the rush, no glow effects, no extra objects, never show a still photo. No subtitles.
  • Frames in pairs — a start and an end state for every shot that changes.
  • Motion reference — the Blender previs drives timing and camera.
  • One take — the rush happens in the same shot as the sip, not after a cut.
  • Sound — off; music, ambience and effects are built in the mix.
07 Edit in After Effects

Finished in After Effects, not in the generator.

The master comp, exported from the real project: 31 layers, each named for what it is — every shot with its in and out, a grade per scene, the push into the can with its keyframes, the time remap that slows the last shot under the end card, and the sound stems. The film is rendered from this project, not stitched outside.

The cut

Trimmed, not regenerated

The can’s pop-in at the start of a take, a one-second freeze before the lob, a duplicate sip — cut out in the edit instead of spending another generation.

24 → 30 fps

Smooth, but never smeared

Pixel Motion for the calm shots. The fast ones — the push, the rush, the lob — go to 30 fps with repeated frames, so a flying can never melts.

Grade

Four scenes, one film

08 Picture

From 720p takes to a 4K master.

The video model delivers 720p. Every take is upscaled locally with Real-ESRGAN, blended 60/40 with the original so skin keeps its texture instead of turning to wax, then graded and given a fine grain in After Effects. Drag the line to compare.

09 Sound

The music wakes up when Leo does.

The takes come out silent apart from one line, so the sound is built from scratch. Maya’s line is recast with ElevenLabs Voice Changer, which keeps the original timing, so her lips stay in sync.

The score is two cues: a sleepy muted-trumpet groove under the meeting that breathes through a filter on her sigh and sinks to a pulse when the can pops — and a bright one that drops on the exact frame the light turns warm. Five bars come out at the cut into the next morning, so its final chord lands on the logo. Every event has its own sound: 33 effects, each picked from four takes.
